Privacy Policy

F8r · September 19, 2026 · Last updated: October 14, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what F8r (“F8r”, “the game”, “we”, “us”) does with your data. F8r is an auto battler for Android in which you build a team of martial artists and watch it fight. The game is offered by the controller named under Controller.

The short version: when you first open F8r we ask you to confirm that you are 16 or older; after that the game creates a guest account for you on our server, which is how your run is saved. You can turn that guest into an account with your email address and a code we mail to it (there is no password), and you can delete the guest or the account, with everything stored under it, from inside the game. If you send us feedback from the game, we keep what you wrote, and an email address only if you give one so we can answer (see 3.6). Now and then, between a fight and the shop, the game shows a full-screen ad through Google AdMob, and every second round the shop offers a free roll for an ad video you may choose to watch; where the law requires it, the game asks for your consent first, and you can change your choice in its settings at any time (see 3.10). We run no analytics. When something breaks, the game sends an error report to Sentry so we can fix it, which you can switch off in its settings (see 3.5). The game asks Google Firebase, from its first start, whether this version may still be played, whether the game is down or closed for maintenance and whether there is news to show on the main menu; it tells Firebase nothing about you or your runs (see 3.9). We do not sell data.

2. Age requirement

F8r is for players aged 16 and older: from 16 on you can give the consent to advertising that 3.10 asks for yourself (Art. 8 GDPR). The first time you open the game, before anything is sent to our server and before anything about ads is asked, we ask you to confirm that you are at least 16. Without the confirmation the game does not continue and no account is created. Players who confirmed 13 under an earlier version of the game are asked once more.

3. What data we process, and why

3.1 Your guest account

F8r has no offline save. To keep your run, the game creates an anonymous user (a “guest”) on our server the first time it starts after the age confirmation, and signs that guest in on every later start. A guest has no name and no email address. What exists about a guest is:

  • a random user ID, the time it was created and the time it was last used;
  • a session, which our backend provider records with the IP address the session was opened from, the name and version of your operating system, the brand and model of your device, the name and version of the app, and the country derived from the IP address (see Appwrite Cloud for the source of this list).

Purpose and legal basis: providing the game and saving your run (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR, performance of a contract); the session data serves the security of the account and of our service (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our legitimate interest in preventing abuse).

3.2 Your registered account

F8r has no passwords. If you choose Continue with email on the account screen and enter your email address, our server puts the address on your guest, and our backend provider sends a six-digit sign-in code to it (from noreply@appwrite.io, in the language the game speaks). The code is valid for 15 minutes. When you enter it, your guest becomes an account with that address, and the address counts as confirmed. The user ID stays the same, so everything stored under the guest becomes the account's. If the address already belongs to an account, the code signs you in to that account instead, and the guest on this device is left behind; the game says so before it sends the code, whenever the guest has a run or a record to lose.

An address you entered but never confirmed with a code stays on your guest. So that nobody can keep an address that is not theirs, our server takes such an address off a guest when somebody else enters it an hour or more later (it is replaced by a placeholder that reaches nobody), and ends the guest's sessions when somebody else enters it within the hour. Our provider additionally derives from an address whether it belongs to a free, disposable or corporate email service; we do not use these flags. An account created with a password by an earlier version of the game keeps that password's salted hash (Argon2) until the account is deleted; we cannot read it, and the game no longer signs in with it.

We use the email address only to send you sign-in codes and to answer a request you send us from it. We send no newsletters and no notifications.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of a contract).

Everything you do in the game is stored under your user ID on our server, because the server, not the phone, decides the outcome of every fight. This is:

Your last fightsBoth teams as they entered the fight (the opponent being another player's team as described in the next row, or a team shopped by the game's own bot), the seed, the fights lost in a row at that point, the outcome, hearts lost, trophies gained, and the time. There are no leaderboards, no live matches against other players, no chat and no usernames. The only thing of yours another player ever sees is a team you sent into a fight, without your name or any other identifier.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR for the run, the fights, your record and the teams; Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR for the divergence reports (our legitimate interest in a game that plays the same on the phone and on the server).

3.4 Technical logs of our backend

Every request the game makes to our server function is recorded by our provider as an execution log: the method and path of the request, the time, the duration, the result status and some request headers. Request and response bodies are not logged. Our provider also keeps an audit log of account events (creation, sign-in, deletion) with the user, the event, the IP address, the location derived from it and the time. We read these logs only to find and fix errors and to investigate abuse.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our legitimate interest in a service that works and is not abused).

3.5 Error reports

When something goes wrong, the game sends an error report to Sentry, from its first start on, so before the age confirmation too. While nothing goes wrong, nothing is sent. A report is one of these:

  • a crash of the app or an error the game did not expect: the error message and the place in the code where it happened (the stack trace);
  • an error our server answered a request with (which route, which error code, and the stack trace);
  • a step of the start that failed (which step, and the error);
  • a divergence report as described in 3.3.

Each report also holds:

  • short lines about what the game was doing before it: that the connection to our server was lost or came back, that the server refused a request (which route, which error code), that a guest or an account signed in (only its kind, never the address), that an ad or the consent form of 3.10 could not be loaded or shown (with the error code the ad library gave). These lines are kept on your phone and travel only with the next report, never on their own;
  • your user id on our server (the random id of your guest or account, see 3.1), once one exists, so we can find the fights and divergences a report is about; before that, a random identifier Sentry's library creates for this installation of the app;
  • what Sentry's library records about the device by itself: make and model, operating system and its version, whether the phone is rooted or jailbroken, the screen's size, the memory, the free storage and the battery level, the device's language and time zone, and the app's version and the libraries it is built with.

A report never holds your email address, a sign-in code, anything you typed, your screen or what you tapped; the game does not count your sessions, measure how you play or record the screen. The game does not put your IP address into a report, and we have set Sentry not to store the address a report arrives from. Of what the game and our server send each other a report holds only the route and error code of a refused request and the divergence report named above. Until a report is sent, the game keeps it in its private storage on your phone (at most 30 reports), so a report written without a connection is sent at a later start.

Switching them off: open Settings and switch Reports on to Reports off under Server. From that moment the game sends nothing more to Sentry (a report already recorded on your phone may still be sent), and from the next start Sentry's library is not started at all. Uninstalling the app ends all reports at once.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our legitimate interest in finding and fixing errors that break the game for its players). You can object at any time (Art. 21 GDPR) with the switch above or by writing to us.

3.6 Feedback you send us

From the menu, from the menu in the shop and from the rating prompt (see 3.8), you can send us feedback: one to five stars, a comment if you like, and an email address only if you want an answer. Nothing is sent until you press Send. With what you wrote, the game sends where you are in it: the round, the fighter set and the difficulty of the run on screen, whether you play on Android or iOS, and the content version of the app. Our server stores all of this in our database (Appwrite, Frankfurt) together with your user id (see 3.1) and the time.

We read feedback to improve the game, and we use an address only to answer the message it came with, never for anything else. Please put nothing in the comment about yourself or others that the feedback does not need. A player can send at most five messages a day.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our legitimate interest in hearing from players and improving the game, and in answering you where you ask us to). Sending feedback is voluntary.

3.7 What we do not collect

F8r contains no analytics library (the error reports of 3.5 are about errors, not about how you play) and no advertising library but Google's, described in 3.10. It uses no push notifications and asks you for no permission. Besides network access (which includes seeing whether the phone has a network at all, so that a server that does not answer is not called a lost connection; that is read on the phone and sent nowhere), the app declares only what Google's ad library brings with it: access to the advertising ID and to Android's ad services (see 3.10). We do not collect your location, contacts, photos, camera, microphone, health or payment data. There are no in-app purchases. Should any of this change, this policy and the Data safety section on Google Play will be updated before the change ships.

3.8 Google Play

You install F8r through Google Play. What Google collects when you do so (for example your Google account, device information and installation statistics) is governed by Google's Privacy Policy; we receive only aggregate, anonymous statistics from the Play Console.

Now and then, back in the shop after a won fight, the game asks whether you would rate it: from your sixth fight on, at most once a day, not for 30 days after you sent us feedback, never again once you went to rate, and not at all after you answered Later three times. Rate F8r opens the game's page on Google Play; the game itself sends nothing when you press it. A rating or review you leave there is published by Google under your Google account and governed by Google's terms; we see it in the Play Console and may answer it there.

3.9 Notices about the app itself (Google Firebase)

F8r asks Google Firebase (its Remote Config service) whether there is something every player has to be told about the app itself; today that is whether this version may still be played or has to be updated from the store first, whether the game, or a part of it such as the fights or the accounts, is down for the moment or closed for planned maintenance (and until when), and whether there is a short note to show on the main menu (news, a notice about a problem, an event). The app asks when it starts, from its very first start on (so before the age confirmation too: a version that may no longer be played must not be playable at all), again when you come back to it, and while it runs it stays connected to Firebase so such a notice reaches it within a few minutes.

A request holds: a random identifier Firebase creates for this installation of the app (the Firebase installation ID) and a token for it; the app's Firebase ID, package name, version and build number; the version of the operating system; the device's language, region setting and time zone; the version of Firebase's library; and the IP address of the connection. It holds nothing about your guest or account, your runs or anything you do in the game: the game sends Firebase no user id. What comes back is the same for every player of a version, and the game keeps the last answer on your phone. A note on the main menu may carry a link; only when you tap it does your browser open that page, and the game sends it nothing of its own.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our legitimate interest in telling every player at once, even while our own server is down, that a version can no longer be played or that the game is down for the moment or closed for maintenance, and in not letting an outdated version run against a server it no longer fits). The game uses Firebase for nothing else: no Google Analytics, no advertising identifier (the ads of 3.10 do not go through Firebase), no crash reports (those go to Sentry, see 3.5).

3.10 Advertising (Google AdMob)

F8r is free, and ads pay for its servers. Now and then, when a fight's tally has ended and before the shop opens, the game shows a full-screen ad that you close to go on. It comes only every few fights and not in your first fights on this install, never at the end of a run, never when the game starts or when you leave it, and never during a fight. In every second round from the third, the shop also offers a free roll for an ad video: the video plays only if you press that button, and the roll is yours once it has played to the end. Rolling for gold stays open beside it, so you never have to watch one. Whether the round's free roll was taken is kept with your run on our server (see 3.3), so it is taken once; nothing about the video itself is. The ads come from Google AdMob, Google's ad network, through Google's ad library in the app.

Your consent. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the game shows Google's consent form once you are on the main menu, the first time and whenever your choice has to be asked again. The form says what the ads may use and names the ad partners; you can agree, refuse, or choose purpose by purpose. Your choice is kept on your phone by Google's consent library (as a consent string under the IAB's Transparency and Consent Framework) and sent along with each ad request. You can change or withdraw it at any time: Settings, Privacy options under the title, which opens the form again (the button is there wherever the form is required). What happens with each choice:

  • With your consent the ads may use the identifiers on your phone (the advertising ID, the app set ID) and, if you agreed to it, be personalised to your interests;
  • without it Google serves only “limited ads”: ads chosen without personalisation and without identifiers stored on your phone, or no ad at all. The game plays the same either way.

What Google's ad library sends. To fetch, show and count an ad, the library sends Google, over an encrypted connection: the IP address of the connection (from which an approximate location is derived); the identifiers above where your choice allows them; information about the device and the app (model, operating system and its version, language, the app's package name and version); what happens with the ad (that it was shown, closed or tapped, and whether a video was watched to the end); and diagnostic information about the library. Google uses this to deliver and measure ads, to fight fraud and, with your consent, to personalise ads. The game itself gives Google nothing of its own: not your user id, not your runs, fights, team or anything you do in the game, not your email address. We see only aggregate figures in the AdMob console (how many ads were shown and what they earned).

The ads are restricted to content suitable for teenagers (Google's rating “T”), and categories such as gambling, dating and alcohol are blocked in our AdMob account.

Legal basis: where the consent form is required, the use of identifiers on your phone and personalised ads rest on your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, § 25(1) TDDDG), which you can withdraw at any time with effect for the future; limited ads, and the IP address and device information any ad needs to reach your phone, rest on our legitimate interest in paying for the game with ads instead of a price (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, § 25(2) No. 2 TDDDG). Google decides itself how it uses the data of ad requests, as an independent controller under the Google Ads Controller-Controller Data Protection Terms; see Google's Privacy Policy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services. You can also reset or delete the advertising ID in your phone's settings (on Android: Settings, Privacy, Ads), which stops it being used for any app.

4. Data that stays on your device

The app keeps the following on your phone only, in its private storage, and never sends it to us: your settings (volume, haptics, reduced motion, whether the shop gives tips and whether error reports may be sent, and the language you chose on the settings screen; without a choice the game reads your phone's language on the phone to pick its own and stores nothing), which of the shop's tips you have already been given, your age confirmation (the age you confirmed being at least, 16), how many fights you have fought on this install and how you last answered the rating prompt (see 3.8), the same count for the ads and when the last ad was shown (see 3.10), which notes on the main menu you closed (see 3.9), a copy of your current run and your last fights so the game can show them while the server is unreachable (a version of the game built without a server keeps your record of finished runs here too, as described in 3.3), the session token that signs your guest or account in, and the Firebase installation ID and the last answer about the app (see 3.9). Google's consent library keeps your choice about ads on the phone too, and sends it with every ad request rather than to us (see 3.10). Uninstalling the app removes all of it. Uninstalling does not delete your account on the server; see Deleting your account.

5. Where the data is stored (Appwrite Cloud, Sentry, Google Firebase, Google AdMob)

Our backend runs on Appwrite Cloud, a service of Appwrite Code Ltd. (privacy policy). Appwrite processes your data on our behalf as a processor under a data processing agreement (Art. 28 GDPR). We use the Frankfurt (FRA) region: all data at rest, including database rows, sessions, logs and backups, stays in Germany; Appwrite states that regions are isolated and data remains within the selected region. The sign-in codes (3.2) are mailed by Appwrite's own mail service on our behalf. Appwrite's own sub-processors are listed in its data processing agreement.

The error reports (3.5) go to Sentry, a service of Functional Software, Inc., 45 Fremont Street, 8th Floor, San Francisco, CA 94105, USA (privacy policy), which processes them on our behalf under its data processing addendum (Art. 28 GDPR). We use Sentry's EU region: the reports are stored in Frankfurt, Germany. Sentry may still access them from the USA, for example to support or secure the service; Functional Software, Inc. is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (Art. 45 GDPR), and its data processing addendum includes the EU standard contractual clauses (Art. 46 GDPR). Feedback (3.6) does not go to Sentry; it is stored by our server at Appwrite like the rest of your data.

The questions about the app itself (3.9) go to Google Firebase (Remote Config and Firebase Installations), services of Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland (privacy and security in Firebase), which processes them on our behalf under the Firebase Data Processing and Security Terms (Art. 28 GDPR). Google may also process them in the USA (Google LLC); Google LLC is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (Art. 45 GDPR), and the terms include the EU standard contractual clauses (Art. 46 GDPR).

The ad requests of 3.10 go to Google AdMob, a service of Google Ireland Limited (the address above). Google is not our processor for them: it processes them as an independent controller under the Google Ads Controller-Controller Data Protection Terms, and may do so in the USA (Google LLC, certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, Art. 45 GDPR; the terms include the EU standard contractual clauses, Art. 46 GDPR). With your consent the ad partners named in the consent form receive data of an ad request too, each under its own privacy policy, which the form links to.

8. Deleting your account and data

You can delete your guest or your registered account, and with it everything stored under it on our server, from inside the game: open the menu, choose Account, then Delete my data (as a guest) or Delete account (with an account), and confirm with Delete. The server then deletes your run, your fights, your record of finished runs, the teams you sent into fights, your divergence reports and the feedback you sent, and finally the account itself with its email address, password hash and sessions. This happens at once and cannot be undone. The game then continues with a fresh, empty guest on your device.

If you have a registered account and no longer have the app, email us from the address of the account at devaseyori@gmail.com with the subject “Delete my F8r account”; we delete it within 30 days and confirm by email. A guest can only be deleted from the game, because nothing except the app on your device identifies a guest to us; a guest that has not been used for a year is removed by our clean-up.

Step-by-step instructions, and what is kept for how long afterwards, are on our account deletion page.

9. Security

All traffic between the app and our server is encrypted in transit (HTTPS/TLS). Passwords are stored as salted Argon2 hashes. Access to the server's tables is restricted to our server function and to us; the app itself can only reach the function, and the function only ever answers the signed-in user about their own data. Our provider holds a SOC 2 Type II attestation. No system is perfectly secure, and we will inform you and the supervisory authority as the GDPR requires should a breach affect your data.

10. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access your data (Art. 15), to have it rectified (Art. 16), erased (Art. 17) or its processing restricted (Art. 18), to receive it in a portable format (Art. 20), and to object to processing that rests on our legitimate interests (Art. 21). We make no automated decisions about you. To exercise a right, email devaseyori@gmail.com; for a registered account, write from the account's address so we can match the request to you. Erasure you can do yourself at any time in the game, see above.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority (Art. 77 GDPR), in particular in the EU member state of your residence. A list of the German supervisory authorities is published by the Federal Commissioner for Data Protection at bfdi.bund.de.

Where we ask for your consent (the ads of 3.10), you can withdraw it at any time, with effect for the future, in the game: Settings, Privacy options. Withdrawing it does not make what happened before unlawful. Requests about the data Google holds from ad requests go to Google, which answers them as their controller (see Google's Privacy Policy); we hold none of it.

11. Children

F8r is not directed at children or at players under 16, and we do not knowingly process their data; the age confirmation at first start is there to keep them out. Should we learn that a player under 16 is playing, we delete the account and everything under it. Parents or guardians who believe this has happened can write to devaseyori@gmail.com.
